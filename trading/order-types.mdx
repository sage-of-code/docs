## Order Types Overview

LiquidLogos supports multiple order types for different trading strategies.

### Market Orders

**Market orders execute immediately at the current market price.**

#### Use Cases
- You want immediate execution
- You're entering a position during high volatility
- You're closing a position quickly

#### Pros
- Instant execution
- Guaranteed fill
- Simple to understand

#### Cons
- Higher slippage on large orders
- No price guarantee (market may move between request and execution)

#### Example
```
Price: $50,000
You buy 1 BTC at market
Executed at: $50,020 (best ask available)
```

### Limit Orders

**Limit orders execute only at your specified price or better.**

#### Use Cases
- You want a specific entry price
- You're trading during low liquidity
- You want to capture a specific support/resistance level

#### Pros
- Price certainty
- Lower slippage
- Can set price and wait

#### Cons
- May not execute if price doesn't reach your level
- Partial fills possible on large orders

#### Example
```
Price: $50,000
You place limit buy at: $49,500
Waits for price to drop
Executes when price reaches $49,500
```

### Stop Orders

**Stop orders trigger when price reaches a level, then execute as market orders.**

#### Use Cases
- Setting stop-loss on existing positions
- Entering on breakouts
- Limiting losses below certain levels

#### Pros
- Automates risk management
- Prevents emotional decisions
- Executes on your terms

#### Cons
- Still executes as market order (slippage possible)
- May not trigger if price gaps past your stop

#### Example
```
You're long at $50,000
You set stop-loss at: $48,000
Price drops to $48,000
Order triggers and market-sells at best available price
```

### Stop-Limit Orders

**Stop-limit combines stop triggers with limit execution.**

#### Use Cases
- Protecting positions with guaranteed prices
- Precise entry points on breakouts
- Limiting slippage on stop orders

#### Pros
- Price protection
- Prevents market impact
- Precise control

#### Cons
- May not execute if price gaps past limit
- More complex to understand

#### Example
```
You're long at $50,000
Set stop at $48,000 with limit at $47,800
Price drops to $48,000, triggers order
Only executes if price ≤ $47,800
```

## Take-Profit and Stop-Loss

### Take-Profit Orders

Automatically close a profitable position at your target price.

**Setting up:**
1. Open a position
2. Go to Positions page
3. Click **Set Take-Profit**
4. Enter your target price
5. Confirm

When price reaches your target, the position closes at market price.

### Stop-Loss Orders

Limit losses by closing at a maximum loss level.

**Setting up:**
1. Open a position
2. Go to Positions page
3. Click **Set Stop-Loss**
4. Enter your maximum loss price
5. Confirm

When price reaches your stop level, the position closes at market price.

## Order Management

### Viewing Open Orders

Go to **Orders** to see:

- Active limit and stop orders
- Pending positions
- Order history

### Canceling Orders

Click **Cancel** on any unfilled order to remove it. You can open a new order immediately.

### Modifying Orders

To change an existing order:

1. Cancel the current order
2. Create a new order with updated parameters

There's no modify function; canceling and recreating is required.

## Tips for Order Management

1. **Use limits during low volatility** — Better prices when market is quiet
2. **Use market orders during high volatility** — Ensure execution
3. **Place stops immediately after entering** — Don't wait; automate your risk
4. **Monitor partially filled orders** — You may need to cancel and resubmit
5. **Avoid gaps** — Don't set stops/targets at psychological levels (everyone does)

## Fee Implications

Different order types may have different fees:

- **Market orders** — May have slightly higher fees
- **Limit orders** — May have slightly lower fees if not filled immediately
- **Stop/Stop-limit** — Same as market once triggered

Check market details for exact fee schedules.

## Advanced Strategies

### Scaling In
Place multiple limit orders at different price levels to enter gradually.

### Pyramiding
Add to winning positions using limit orders at progressively higher prices.

### Hedging
Use opposite-direction orders to hedge existing positions.

## Troubleshooting

**Order not executing?**
- Check your margin ratio (must have sufficient collateral)
- Verify your limit price is competitive
- Ensure order wasn't auto-cancelled

**Order partially filled?**
- Large orders may fill across multiple fills
- You can cancel the unfilled remainder

**Price gapped past my stop?**
- Gap fills happen during high volatility
- Consider tighter stops or larger stop distances
